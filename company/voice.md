# Voice — Future Works

How to write on behalf of this client. Read before drafting any copy.

Source: "Voice rules for vendors" and "Language rules" sections of the R11 Positioning, Lead Magnets, Elevator Pitches and Mission/Values documents; ICP doc (27 Oct 2026) for proof-line rules.

---

## Brand Voice

**Overall tone:** Confident, plain-spoken, specific, expertise-forward. "Write like a sharp operator explaining something, not a billboard."
**Personality:** Candid, outcome-obsessed, senior. Direct and honest, "bad news travels fast", "humor stays", "no consultant hedging".
**What to avoid:** Billboard / hype tone, buzzwords, consultant hedging, hollow superlatives, anything that sounds like a vendor painting AI on decks.

**Formula from the client:** lead with the buyer's problem, quantify the outcome, name the mechanism. For descriptions of Future Works: category line first, then mechanism, then one proof point.

---

## Writing Rules

- **Company name is "Future Works"**, two words, always.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, colons or parentheses.
- **Spelling: US English** (client is US-facing: "mobilize", "optimization", "realized").
- Anchor every claim to a proof point. "If you cannot cite one, do not make the claim."
- **Keep figures out of the first cold email.** Use proof lines only where the sequence allows a proof line.
- **Keep Philips and NextPower anonymous** in outbound, decks, landing pages and any vendor-built material (use the approved anonymous lines below).
- No prices, percentage comparisons or the word "cheaper" on public channels. Commercial detail stays in commercial conversations.
- No named-competitor comparisons on homepage or hero surfaces (named comparisons only on dedicated pages after legal review). In cold email, do not name competitors.
- Do not publicly name proprietary frameworks or internal codenames (including persona codenames like Gamora / Star-Lord). Describe the technology instead.
- Keep size framing (employee and revenue floors) out of public copy.
- Do not describe certifications as held: SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 are "audits in progress and on schedule".
- Industries are proof, not the offer: lead with the problem and the capability, then anchor with the closest proof point.

## Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Replace with |
|-------|-------------|
| "asset-heavy operators" / "asset-heavy enterprises" | Nothing; use the category line or name the industries ("we work across manufacturing, supply chains, energy, and health") |
| "AI-powered" (with no specifics) | The specific mechanism or outcome |
| "revolutionary", "game-changing", "best-in-class", "disrupt" | A specific claim with a proof point |
| "synergy" | Remove |
| "leverage" (as a verb) | "use" |
| "not just X, it is Y" construction; hollow superlatives | A plain statement |
| "digital transformation" | "AI-native transformation" |
| web design, website development, app development, UI UX, digital product, future of AI, innovation strategy | Retired terms; do not use |
| Vertical-as-offer phrasing ("real estate solutions", "aerospace digital transformation") | Capability first, industry as proof |
| "Enterprise" in front of capability names | "AI Operationalization", "Agentic Workflow Execution" |
| "cheaper" / prices / percentage price comparisons | Risk-absorption framing |
| "ROI or We Pay", "Free Until Value Pilot", "Twelve-Month Hypercare" | Retired commitments; use the outcome-staked fee language |
| Em dash (—) | Comma, period, colon or parentheses |

Also banned by this OS (CLAUDE.md): "leverage", "synergy", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "hope this helps".

## Approved Claims

Claims the client has explicitly approved for use (all from client documents; "the numbers are signed"). Not in Email 1.

- "Future Works delivers in twelve weeks what incumbents quote at two years."
- "A third of every fee is staked on a value target your Finance team validates." (Service credit if missed, bonus if exceeded.)
- "A global health technology company saw a 2-6x return in twelve weeks, signed off by its own Finance team, and funded the next cycle from the result." / short: "a global health technology company (2-6x return in twelve weeks, Finance-signed)"
- "At a global energy equipment manufacturer, the first twelve-week cycle took more than 20,000 work hours a year off the operation. The client has extended every cycle since, more than twelve so far." / short: "a global energy equipment manufacturer (more than 20,000 work hours a year removed in cycle one)"
- "75% faster time to market" (energy equipment manufacturer)
- "JLL: a two-year AI roadmap unlocked in eight weeks" ({{TO CONFIRM}}: Sep docs say four weeks)
- "Staples: twenty digital products in twelve weeks"
- "Boom: engineering workflows agentified and shipped into production"
- "BCG's research: 74% of enterprises show no measurable value from AI investment" (problem stat)
- "GDPR-compliant delivery, with SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certification audits in progress and on schedule. Built on AWS, Azure, and Databricks."

**Do not use claims not on this list without checking first.** Not approved for outbound: "Philips 3x" and named Philips/NextPower references (superseded by the anonymous lines in the 27 Oct ICP doc).

## Examples

### Good example
Client-supplied boilerplate:

> Future Works is the AI-native transformation engine. We rebuild the operations that move the P&L (manufacturing, energy, health and life sciences, logistics, and supply chain) in twelve-week cycles run by named senior experts and fleets of AI agents. Our fees are staked on outcomes, verified by our clients' own finance teams. We deliver in twelve weeks what incumbents quote at two years.

1-minute pitch opening (client-supplied): "Most AI dies in pilots. Most transformation programs run for two years before anyone sees value on the P&L. We fix both."

### Bad example
> "Future Works is a revolutionary, AI-powered partner for asset-heavy operators — not just a consultancy, it's a game-changer for your digital transformation."

Why it fails: hype words, "AI-powered" with no specifics, retired "asset-heavy operators", em dash, "not just X, it is Y", retired "digital transformation".
